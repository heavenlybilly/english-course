## Task 1. Recall Pronunciation Tools

1. Words carrying the message usually stand out more.
2. Possible reasons include contractions, reduction, linking, unfamiliar stress or a sound contrast.
3. It highlights important information and helps the listener follow the message.

## Task 4. Find the Evidence

1. `Busy as usual.`
2. `I never have time to see a movie.`
3. `Friday nights are when I visit my parents.`
4. `I always jog. Well, sometimes I jog. Okay, I never jog.`
5. `It looks like we'll never have time to see a movie.`
6. `I'll do my errands on Sunday.`

Minor differences in punctuation do not matter if the evidence is correct.

## Task 5. Match Spoken and Written Forms

1. `What is`.
2. `want to`.
3. `we will`.
4. `going to`.
5. Possible answers include `I'm — I am`, `it's — it is`, `I'll — I will` and `that's — that is`.

## Task 6. Recover the Sound Stream

1. `want to`.
2. `we will`.
3. `going to`.
4. `I will`.

The official transcript represents the first and third expressions as `wanna` and `gonna` because it records informal speech. The completed answers show the standard forms for ordinary writing.

## Task 7. Check What Became Clearer

Open. Give specific evidence from the recording. A useful answer names a phrase or detail and explains which step helped.

## Task 8. Retell the Decision

**Possible response**

> Anna and Marsha want to see a movie. Anna is tap dancing on Thursday, and Marsha visits her parents on Friday. They mention several other activities, including jogging and playing board games. On Saturday afternoon, they realize that the movie starts in thirty minutes. Marsha decides to do her errands on Sunday, so they go to the movie immediately.

Other accurate retellings are possible.

## Task 9. Select a Response

1. Listen again with one question: `When do they finally go?`
2. Listen for the speakers, activity and purpose; use a shorter section if necessary.
3. Compare the short phrase with the transcript, notice the reduction and listen again.
4. Ignore it for now or check it later.
5. Hide the transcript and listen again; revisit only a short difficult section if needed.

## Task 10. Reconstruct the Method

The useful order is **C → E → A → F → D → B**:

1. listen for the main idea;
2. listen for selected details;
3. inspect the transcript;
4. notice useful language or connected speech;
5. listen again without the transcript;
6. respond to the content.

The transcript is not stage one because reading supplies the words before the learner has tried to recognize them in speech.

## Task 11. Identify the Main Problem

**A.** Anna forgets Marsha's birthday and makes a gift with borrowed office supplies.

The speakers are coworkers and friends.

## Task 12. Follow the Borrowing

1. Marsha's birthday celebration at a restaurant.
2. Anna does not receive her paycheck until the following week.
3. A stapler.
4. Scissors.
5. The next day.
6. An office-supply hat.
7. Any four: paper, pens, tape, erasers, rubber bands, binder clips, paper clips, a light and a balloon.
8. It helps Marsha's friends find her in the restaurant.

## Task 13. Check the Evidence and Phrases

1. See Task 12.
2. `I don't get my paycheck until next week.`
3. `May I borrow your stapler?` and `Can I borrow your scissors?`
4. `I can lend you my stapler` and `I loaned my stapler to the wrong person.`
5. `I'll bring them back tomorrow.`
6. `I gotta go!` It represents an informal pronunciation of `I've got to go` or, in meaning, `I have to go`.

## Task 14. Explain the Solution

**Possible response**

> Anna forgets her friend's birthday and cannot buy a gift before her next paycheck. She borrows a stapler, scissors and several other office supplies from coworkers. She uses them to make an office-supply hat. Marsha thinks the gift is unusual, but she accepts it. I think the solution is creative, although the hat is not very practical.

Other accurate responses and opinions are possible.

## Task 15. Work with Another Short Recording

Check that the source can be found again and that every stage was completed in order. The first main-idea note must come from listening without text. Detail questions should ask for information that is actually present. The final response should react to meaning rather than only describe pronunciation.

## Task 16. Make a Micro-Transcript

Check the attempt against the official text. The classification should explain the likely cause of each important difference. More than one cause may apply. Select one manageable feature for another listen rather than trying to fix every difference at once.
