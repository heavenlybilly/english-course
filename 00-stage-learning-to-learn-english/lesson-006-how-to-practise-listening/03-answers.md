# Lesson 006 — Answers

## Task 1. Recall Pronunciation Tools

1. The following syllable has primary stress.
2. Words carrying the main message usually stand out more.
3. It highlights important information and helps the listener follow the message.

## Task 2. Choose the Main Idea

**B.** One friend changes the meeting time, but the activity stays the same.

1. Two people speak.
2. Informally.
3. No. Their situation, change and final decision are clear without analyzing every word.

6. **False.** They agree to meet outside the café.

## Task 4. Find the Evidence

1. `I hope so`.
2. `Could we meet after lunch instead?`
3. `open until four, so we still have time`.
4. `again`.
5. `Let's meet outside the café`.

## Task 5. Match What You Hear to What Is Written

1. `have to`.
2. `could`.
3. `I would`.
4. `I will`.
5. **send** and **address**. Depending on the contrast, another word can receive special prominence.

## Task 6. Mark the Message Words

**Suggested answers**

1. `MEET`, `AFTER LUNCH`, `INSTEAD`.
2. `REPAIR CAFÉ`, `OPEN`, `FOUR`.
3. `STOPPED WORKING`, `AGAIN`.
4. `SEND`, `ADDRESS`.

Natural speakers may distribute prominence somewhat differently. The choices should make the intended information easy to follow.

## Task 7. Check What Became Clearer

Open. Give specific evidence from the recording. A useful answer names a phrase or detail and explains which step helped.

## Task 8. Retell the Plan

**Possible response**

> Maya has to finish a report, so she wants to meet later. She and Leo will meet outside the repair café at two thirty. Maya will bring her desk lamp because it has stopped working again. Leo will send her the address.

Other clear retellings are possible.

## Task 9. Select a Response

1. Listen again with one question: `What time will they meet?`
2. Listen for the speakers, place, topic and purpose; a shorter section may help.
3. Hide the transcript and listen again. If necessary, compare one short difficult phrase before trying again.
4. Ignore it for now or check it later.
5. Choose a short section and give it a specific listening purpose.

## Task 10. Reconstruct the Method

The useful order is **C → E → A → F → D → B**:

1. listen for the main idea;
2. listen for selected details;
3. inspect the transcript;
4. notice useful language or connected speech;
5. listen again without the transcript;
6. respond to the meaning.

The transcript is not stage one because reading supplies the words before the learner has tried to recognize them in speech.

3. `Friday`.
4. `six` / `6:00`.
5. `four` / `4:00`.
6. `card`.

## Task 13. Check and Notice

1. See Task 12.
2. `We can hold it until Friday evening.`
3. `on weekdays`.
4. **HOLD**, **FRIDAY EVENING**; **we** could stand out in a special contrast, but it is not necessary here.
5. These details determine who the message is for, when action is required and when collection is possible. Small grammar words support the sentences but do not carry the core practical information.

## Task 14. Leave a Useful Note

**Possible response**

> Daniel, your English pronunciation guide is ready at West Street Library. They will keep it until Friday evening. The library closes at six on weekdays and four on Saturday. Bring your library card.

The phone number is useful only if Daniel no longer needs the book. A shorter accurate note is acceptable.

## Task 15. Work with a Short Recording

Check that the source can be found again and that every stage was completed in order. The first main-idea note must come from listening without text. Detail questions should ask for information that is actually present. The final response should react to meaning rather than only describe pronunciation.

## Task 16. Make a Micro-Transcript

Check the attempt against the official text. The classification should explain the likely reason for each important difference. More than one cause may apply; for example, an unfamiliar word may also be difficult to hear because it is unstressed. Select one manageable feature for another listen rather than trying to fix every difference at once.
