## Your Goal

Learn a repeatable listening process with a real recording: listen first for the situation and main idea, listen again for selected details, inspect the official English transcript, notice a small amount of spoken language and finish with one more listen without text.

Understanding does not mean hearing every word. A successful first listen may give you only the speakers, situation and purpose.

### Task 1. Recall Pronunciation Tools

Without reopening Lesson 005, answer briefly.

1. Which words usually stand out more: words carrying the message or small grammar words?
2. Name one reason why familiar words may be difficult to recognize in speech.
3. How can sentence stress help a listener?

## Use a Listening Sequence

Use this order for short intensive listening:

1. Read the task, not the transcript.
2. Listen once for the situation and main idea.
3. Listen again for selected details.
4. Inspect the English transcript and resolve important gaps.
5. Notice a few useful phrases or features of connected speech.
6. Listen again without the transcript.
7. Respond to the content.

Do not open the official transcript before Tasks 2 and 3. Reading early changes the activity into a reading task.

## Notice Everyday Spoken English

The conversation includes informal reductions and contractions that are common in speech.

#### Spoken-Language Clues

| Spoken form | Full or more neutral form | What to notice |
|---|---|---|
| `What's going on?` | `What is going on?` | the contraction is brief |
| `Do you wanna see a movie?` | `Do you want to see a movie?` | **want to** is reduced in informal speech |
| `I'm` | `I am` | the vowel and /m/ attach to the pronoun |
| `we'll` | `we will` | the auxiliary is reduced |
| `gonna start` | `going to start` | common informal spoken reduction |
| `I'll` | `I will` | one short syllable |

`Wanna` and `gonna` help you recognize casual speech. In ordinary course writing, use `want to` and `going to` unless you are deliberately representing informal speech.

### Task 5. Match Spoken and Written Forms

1. What is the full form of `What's`?
2. Which words are represented by `wanna`?
3. What is the full form of `we'll`?
4. Which phrase is reduced to `gonna`?
5. Find one more contraction in the official transcript and write its full form.

### Task 6. Recover the Sound Stream

Listen to the relevant lines again and complete the standard written forms.

1. Do you `_____` see a movie with me?
2. It looks like `_____` never have time to see a movie.
3. The new movie is `_____` start in thirty minutes.
4. `_____` do my errands on Sunday.

Then read each complete sentence aloud as one or two meaningful groups, not as isolated words.

## Return to Listening Without Text

Hide the transcript and listen to the complete Conversation audio once more.

### Task 7. Check What Became Clearer

1. Name one phrase that is easier to recognize now.
2. Name one detail you missed before reading the transcript.
3. Can you follow the schedule without translating every sentence into Russian?
4. What helped most: another listen, the transcript, or analysis of a reduced form? Explain briefly.

The goal is not to make every sound equally clear. It is to connect the sound stream with phrases and meaning.

## Respond to the Content

### Task 8. Retell the Decision

Without copying the transcript, write or say 4–6 sentences explaining:

- what Anna and Marsha want to do;
- why Thursday and Friday do not work;
- one other activity mentioned;
- when they finally decide to go;
- what Marsha changes in her schedule.

Use simple language. Exact wording from the recording is not required.

## Choose the Right Tool for the Problem

| Listening problem | Useful response |
|---|---|
| You do not know the situation | Listen for speakers, place, topic and purpose |
| You know the main idea but miss one fact | Listen again with one detail question |
| You know the words on paper but cannot hear them | Compare one short section with the transcript |
| One word is unfamiliar but unimportant | Ignore it or check it later |
| The recording feels too long | Work with a clearly defined short section |
| You understand only after reading | Hide the transcript and listen again |

### Task 9. Select a Response

Choose the most useful next action.

1. You know they are arranging a movie, but you missed which day finally works.
2. You cannot identify the topic after one full listen.
3. You understand `going to start` on paper but hear only something like `gonna start`.
4. One unknown game name does not affect the main idea.
5. You understand the transcript but still cannot follow the audio alone.
