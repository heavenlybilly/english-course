## Your Goal

Learn a repeatable listening process: listen first for the situation and main idea, listen again for useful details, inspect the transcript, notice selected features of connected speech and finish with one more listen without the transcript.

Understanding does not mean hearing every word. A successful first listen can give you only the situation, speakers and general purpose.

### Task 1. Recall Pronunciation Tools

1. What does `/ˈ/` show in an IPA transcription?
2. Which words usually stand out more in a sentence: words carrying the main message or small grammar words?
3. Why can sentence stress help a listener?

## Use a Listening Sequence

Use this order for short intensive listening:

1. Read the task, not the transcript.
2. Listen once for the situation and main idea.
3. Listen again for selected details.
4. Inspect the transcript and resolve important gaps.
5. Notice a small amount of useful language or connected speech.
6. Listen again without the transcript.
7. Respond to the meaning.

Do not read the transcript before Tasks 2 and 3. The first two listens train listening; reading early changes the task into reading.

## Notice Why Speech Sounds Different

Written words do not stay separate and equally strong in natural speech. Speakers link words, reduce unstressed words and use contractions.

#### Connected-Speech Clues

| Written form | A common natural pronunciation | What to notice |
|---|---|---|
| `are we` | /ər wi/ | **are** is weak |
| `have to` | often sounds close to `hafta` | /v/ changes before /t/ |
| `could we` | /kəd wi/ | **could** may contain schwa |
| `I'd like to` | the words link in one group | the contraction is brief |
| `let's meet` | final /s/ links toward **meet** | no full pause between words |
| `I'll send you` | **I'll** is short; **send** is prominent | message words stand out |

Spellings such as `hafta` are listening descriptions, not forms to use in ordinary writing.

## Return to Listening Without the Transcript

Hide or scroll away from the transcript. Listen once more.

### Task 7. Check What Became Clearer

1. Name one phrase that is easier to hear now.
2. Name one detail that you missed before reading the transcript.
3. Can you follow the decision without translating each sentence into Russian?
4. Which helped most: another listen, the transcript, or noticing a sound pattern? Explain briefly.

The goal is not to make every sound equally clear. It is to connect the sound stream with words, phrases and meaning.

## Choose the Right Tool for the Problem

Different listening problems need different responses.

| Problem | Useful response |
|---|---|
| You do not know the situation | Listen for speakers, place, topic and purpose |
| You know the main idea but miss one fact | Listen again with one detail question |
| You know the words on paper but cannot hear them | Compare a short section with the transcript |
| One key word is unfamiliar | Guess from context, then check if it controls meaning |
| The whole recording feels too fast | Use a shorter section; do not analyze everything at once |
| You understand the text after reading | Hide the transcript and listen again |

### Task 9. Select a Response

Choose the most useful next action.

1. You understand that two people are arranging a meeting, but you missed the time.
2. You cannot identify the topic after one full listen.
3. You understand every sentence with the transcript but not without it.
4. One unknown adjective does not affect the main idea.
5. A ten-minute recording feels impossible to analyze closely.
