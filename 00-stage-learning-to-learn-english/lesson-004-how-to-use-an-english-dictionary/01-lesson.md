## Decide Whether to Look It Up

Stopping at every unfamiliar word makes reading slow and breaks the flow. Before opening a dictionary, ask what you need to do.

#### Four Possible Decisions

- **Ignore:** the word is not necessary for your purpose.
- **Guess:** the context gives you a useful temporary idea.
- **Check later:** you can finish the passage first and return to the word.
- **Look up now:** the word controls the key meaning or the task asks you to use it accurately.

A guess is not a fact. Check it before you use the word in your own sentence.

#### Short Text: A Late Delivery

> Nina ordered a desk lamp online. The store promised delivery on Tuesday, but the parcel did not arrive. On Wednesday, the tracking page said that delivery had been **postponed** until Friday. Nina needed the lamp for work, so she contacted the store and asked whether she could collect it herself.

### Task 2. Choose the Right Action

## Read Only the Part You Need

A dictionary entry may contain several meanings. Do not automatically choose the first translation. Use the sentence, part of speech and grammar around the word.

#### Simplified Dictionary Entry: `light`

> **light** /laɪt/  
> **noun [C/U]** — brightness that lets you see: *The room gets plenty of natural light.*  
> **noun [C]** — a lamp or other source of light: *Turn off the kitchen light.*  
> **adjective** — not heavy: *This bag is light and easy to carry.*  
> **adjective** — pale in color: *He was wearing a light blue shirt.*  
> **verb [T]** — make something start to burn: *She lit a candle.*

Common labels:

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `n`, `v`, `adj`, `adv` | noun, verb, adjective, adverb |
| `[C]`, `[U]` | countable, uncountable |
| `[T]`, `[I]` | transitive, intransitive |
| `sb`, `sth` | somebody, something |
| `AmE`, `BrE` | American, British English |
| `formal`, `informal` | register |

Different dictionaries use slightly different labels. Check the dictionary's own guide when a label is unfamiliar.

## Look Beyond the Translation

A translation can help you understand meaning, but it may not show how to build a sentence. Look for the pattern, common combinations and examples.

#### Simplified Dictionary Entry: `depend`

> **depend on/upon sb/sth** — to be decided or influenced by something  
> *The price depends on the size.*  
> *It depends on whether we have enough time.*  
> *You can depend on Maya to keep her promise.*

For most everyday uses, save `depend on something`, not only `depend — зависеть`. The last example has a related meaning: Maya is reliable. You do not need to learn every meaning at once.

#### Useful Patterns from Dictionary Entries

> `ask somebody for something` — I asked the clerk for help.  
> `suggest doing something` — She suggested taking a break.  
> `interested in something` — I'm interested in photography.  
> `advice [U]` — He gave me some useful advice.  
> `borrow something from somebody` — I borrowed a charger from Leo.  
> `lend something to somebody` — Leo lent a charger to me.

Notice what the entry prevents:

- not `depend from the weather`, but `depend on the weather`;
- not `suggest to take`, but `suggest taking`;
- not `an advice`, but `some advice` or `a piece of advice`;
- not `borrow me a charger`, but `lend me a charger`.

## Use Examples as Evidence

One example can be unusual or too narrow. Read two or three examples and look for what repeats. Then create a sentence with the same meaning and pattern.

Suppose you meet **issue** in this sentence:

> We had an issue with the payment, but the bank fixed it.

The relevant entry is:

> **issue noun [C]** — a problem or subject that needs attention  
> *There is an issue with the heating.*  
> *We need to discuss this issue.*  
> *The team is working to resolve the issue.*

Useful combinations include `an issue with something`, `discuss an issue` and `resolve an issue`. In everyday conversation, **problem** is often the simpler choice. **Issue** can sound less direct in some situations.

## Check Pronunciation When It Matters

Dictionary audio and phonemic transcription can answer specific questions. Listen for the stressed syllable and compare related forms.

#### Pronunciation Note

> **record** noun /ˈrekərd/ — **RE**cord  
> **record** verb /rɪˈkɔrd/ — re**CORD**

You do not need to memorize every symbol before using dictionary audio. At this stage, notice the stress and repeat the whole word in a short phrase:

> a useful **record**  
> **record** your voice

## A Practical Dictionary Routine

When a word matters, use this sequence:

1. Keep the original sentence.
2. Identify the likely part of speech.
3. Choose the meaning that fits the context.
4. Check the pattern, labels and two or three examples.
5. Check pronunciation if you plan to say the word or the stress is unclear.
6. Save only what is useful: unit, meaning, pattern and your own example.
7. Close the entry and recall the expression later.

Do not turn every search into a long vocabulary note. A dictionary is a tool for answering questions, not a list that you must copy.
