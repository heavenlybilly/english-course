## Task 1. Recall Useful Chunks

1. **Make.**
2. **to.**
3. **up.**
4. **down.**
5. **out.**
6. **of.**
7. **make.**
8. **up.**

## Task 2. Write Without Stopping

Open initial draft. Check only that a goal and difficulty are understandable; keep gaps for revision. Example: “I want to learn to cook. I get home late and don't know what to make. Can you help me choose an easy meal?”

## Task 3. Find What Makes the Message Clear

1. “Could you suggest something simple that I can make in twenty minutes?”
2. Getting home late and feeling tired.
3. Usually enough for this general request. For buying or borrowing a specific item, the description needs more detail.

## Task 4. Correct Capitals and Punctuation

1. “Hi Sam, I want to learn to cook. Can you suggest one easy meal?”
2. `Hi` starts the message and the sentence; `Sam` is a name; `Can` begins a new question.
3. “I live in Moscow and I study English.” `I`, `Moscow` and `English` require capitals.
4. Suggested: “Hi Anna, can you help me? I need something for my bike.” `Hi` begins the message, `Anna` is a name, and the greeting takes a comma. The request is a question; the final statement takes a period.

## Task 5. Explain the Meaning Simply

1. Suggested: “A place where you can borrow books.” Accept other identifying descriptions; “a place with things” is too vague.
2. “A job I don't like.” / “Work I don't enjoy.”
3. “The task takes a lot of time.”
4. “I keep checking my phone instead of starting my work.” Other examples of delaying a task are valid.
5. Suggested: “It is a small black bag with a red pocket.” Any three useful identifying details are acceptable.

## Task 6. Make Your Message Clearer

Open. Use the feedback criteria below. Example: “Hi Alex, I'd like to learn to cook a few simple meals. I don't have much time in the evening, and I often buy food on my way home. Could you send me a recipe with only a few ingredients? I have a small kitchen, so I can't use lots of equipment. I can make time for one meal on Sunday and try it out then. What would you suggest?”

## Task 7. Ask for a Tool You Cannot Name

1. Suggested: “Hi, could I borrow a tool for making a hole in the wall? I want to put up a small shelf. Do you have something I could use?” Naming a drill is unnecessary. Criteria: clear function, borrowing request, understandable polite wording.
2. Open. Example: “I used ‘a tool for making a hole’ to explain its purpose.” Must identify an actual phrase from the learner's response.

## Feedback for writing

Assess each point as achieved / partly achieved / needs work. This is a feedback aid, not a CEFR certification scale.

- Task: goal, difficulty and request are present where required.
- Meaning: a reader can understand the situation and respond.
- Connection: sentences follow a useful order.
- Language: errors do not obscure the message; circumlocution is specific enough.
- Revision: the learner made an independent revision before requesting external feedback.

Must Fix is a real error. Better concerns naturalness; Style concerns register; Advanced is optional. An alternative to the model is not automatically wrong. Correct the most important obstacles first and rewrite the relevant sentence.

## Task 8. Describe It More Clearly

1. “Something you use to boil water.” Other clear descriptions are valid.
2. “Someone who lives near you.”
3. “I'm not happy with the place where I'm staying.” / “I don't like where I'm living.”
4. Open. Example: “Please bring your small red bag tomorrow. I need it to carry the books.”
5. “I have a job I don't like.”
6. “The small metal thing you put in the lock to open the door.” A handle is the part you hold or turn; the lock detail helps distinguish them.

## Task 9. Write from Memory

Open. Check goal, difficulty, request, clarity and independent first draft. Model: “Hi Jo, I want to learn to take better photos. I use my phone, but the pictures often look too dark. I don't know which settings to change. Could you show me one simple thing to try? I can make time for a short walk this weekend. We could take a few pictures together and compare them. I would like to understand what makes a photo look better.”

## Task 10. Ask a Shop for Help

Open. Check function, identifying detail, fault and request. Model: “Hello, I bought a small white machine from your shop last Friday. It is the one that heats water for tea, with a blue light near the handle. I can't remember the model name, but I have the receipt. It worked on the first day. Now the light comes on, but the water stays cold. Could you tell me whether I should bring it back to the shop? I would like a replacement if possible.” Other coherent descriptions and requests are valid.
