# Lesson 007 — Answers and Feedback

## Lesson

- **1A-1:** Make.
- **1A-2:** to.
- **1A-3:** up.
- **1A-4:** down.
- **1A-5:** out.
- **1A-6:** of.
- **1A-7:** make.
- **1A-8:** up.
- **2A-1:** Open initial draft. Check only that a goal and difficulty are understandable; keep gaps for revision. Example: “I want to learn to cook. I get home late and don't know what to make. Can you help me choose an easy meal?”
- **3A-1:** “Could you suggest something simple that I can make in twenty minutes?”
- **3A-2:** Getting home late and feeling tired.
- **3A-3:** Usually enough for this general request. For buying or borrowing a specific item, the description needs more detail.
- **4A-1:** “Hi Sam, I want to learn to cook. Can you suggest one easy meal?”
- **4A-2:** `Hi` starts the message and the sentence; `Sam` is a name; `Can` begins a new question.
- **4A-3:** “I live in Moscow and I study English.” `I`, `Moscow` and `English` require capitals.
- **4A-4:** Suggested: “Hi Anna, can you help me? I need something for my bike.” `Hi` begins the message, `Anna` is a name, and the greeting takes a comma. The request is a question; the final statement takes a full stop.
- **5A-1:** Suggested: “A place where you can borrow books.” Accept other identifying descriptions; “a place with things” is too vague.
- **5A-2:** “A job I don't like.” / “Work I don't enjoy.”
- **5A-3:** “The task takes a lot of time.”
- **5A-4:** “I keep checking my phone instead of starting my work.” Other examples of delaying a task are valid.
- **5A-5:** Suggested: “It is a small black bag with a red pocket.” Any three useful identifying details are acceptable.
- **6A-1:** Open. Use the feedback criteria below. Example: “Hi Alex, I'd like to learn to cook a few simple meals. I don't have much time in the evening, and I often buy food on my way home. Could you send me a recipe with only a few ingredients? I have a small kitchen, so I can't use lots of equipment. I can make time for one meal on Sunday and try it out then. What would you suggest?”
- **7A-1:** Suggested: “Hi, could I borrow a tool for making a hole in the wall? I want to put up a small shelf. Do you have something I could use?” Naming a drill is unnecessary. Criteria: clear function, borrowing request, understandable polite wording.
- **7A-2:** Open. Example: “I used ‘a tool for making a hole’ to explain its purpose.” Must identify an actual phrase from the learner's response.

## Feedback for writing

Assess each point as achieved / partly achieved / needs work. This is a feedback aid, not a CEFR certification scale.

- Task: goal, difficulty and request are present where required.
- Meaning: a reader can understand the situation and respond.
- Connection: sentences follow a useful order.
- Language: errors do not obscure the message; circumlocution is specific enough.
- Revision: the learner made an independent revision before requesting external feedback.

Must Fix is a real error. Better concerns naturalness; Style concerns register; Advanced is optional. An alternative to the model is not automatically wrong. Correct the most important obstacles first and rewrite the relevant sentence.

## Additional practice

- **P1A-1:** “Something you use to boil water.” Other clear descriptions are valid.
- **P1A-2:** “Someone who lives near you.”
- **P1A-3:** “I'm not happy with the place where I'm staying.” / “I don't like where I'm living.”
- **P1A-4:** Open. Example: “Please bring your small red bag tomorrow. I need it to carry the books.”
- **P1A-5:** “I have a job I don't like.”
- **P1A-6:** “The small metal thing you put in the lock to open the door.” A handle is the part you hold or turn; the lock detail helps distinguish them.
- **P2A-1:** Open. Check goal, difficulty, request, clarity and independent first draft. Model: “Hi Jo, I want to learn to take better photos. I use my phone, but the pictures often look too dark. I don't know which settings to change. Could you show me one simple thing to try? I can make time for a short walk this weekend. We could take a few pictures together and compare them. I would like to understand what makes a photo look better.”
- **P3A-1:** Open. Check function, identifying detail, fault and request. Model: “Hello, I bought a small white machine from your shop last Friday. It is the one that heats water for tea, with a blue light near the handle. I can't remember the model name, but I have the receipt. It worked on the first day. Now the light comes on, but the water stays cold. Could you tell me whether I should bring it back to the shop? I would like a replacement if possible.” Other coherent descriptions and requests are valid.
