# Lesson 002 — Reading Without Translating Every Word

[Previous lesson](../lesson-001-how-we-will-learn-english/01-lesson.md) · [Stage 0](../README.md) · [Additional practice](02-practice.md) · [Answers](03-answers.md)

## 1. Your goal

Read a short story, understand what changes, and choose which unknown words need attention. You do not need to translate every sentence.

## 2. A simple reading strategy and first reading

Before you read, decide what you need to understand. During the first reading:

1. Follow the people, events and changes.
2. Keep reading if an unknown word does not block the main meaning.
3. Use the surrounding sentence to make a tentative guess when useful.
4. Look up a word only if it is necessary for your reading goal.
5. After reading, you may check words that seem useful or important.

Read once without a dictionary. Keep going when one word is unclear. Then answer the two questions without translating the story.

While reading, follow three things: what Maya wants, what happens, and what changes.

### A different kind of repair

On Saturday morning, Maya took a broken desk lamp to a repair café in the local library. She had seen a notice in the window: “Bring something broken. Learn how to repair it.” She liked the idea, but she was not sure what to expect. Would somebody repair the lamp for her, or would she have to do everything herself?

At the entrance, a volunteer gave her a number and asked her to wait. The room was busy. An older man was examining a radio, two teenagers were working on a bicycle, and a woman was sewing a bright blue bag. Nobody seemed to be in a hurry. Maya sat beside a man with a small wooden chair.

“It belonged to my daughter,” he explained. “She's too big for it now, but my grandson could use it.” One of the legs was loose. He could buy a new chair, he said, but that was not really the point.

When Maya's number was called, she met Leo, a volunteer who repaired electrical equipment. He asked what had happened to the lamp. Maya explained that it had stopped working after she moved her desk. She expected him to open it immediately. Instead, he asked her to describe what she had already tried.

Together, they checked the lamp carefully. Leo explained each step, but he also gave Maya time to think. The problem was a damaged switch. They could not replace it that day because they did not have the right part. Maya felt disappointed. She had imagined carrying a working lamp home.

Leo wrote down the name of the part and showed her how to find it. “Come back next month,” he said. “We can finish the job together.”

Outside, Maya passed a shop selling new lamps. Buying one would be faster. Still, she kept walking. Her old lamp was in her bag, exactly as broken as before, but something was different. Now she knew what was wrong with it. More importantly, she wanted to find out whether she could help make it work again.

**2A-1.** Which description fits best?
A. Maya learns that repairing things is always cheaper.
B. Maya starts to see repair as something she can take part in.
C. Maya decides to become a volunteer.

**2A-2.** Is the lamp working when Maya leaves? Give one piece of evidence.

## 3. Read for evidence

Read again. Answer briefly in English or Russian. Point to a phrase that supports your answer.

**3A-1.** What was Maya unsure about before entering?
**3A-2.** Why does the man want to repair the chair?
**3A-3.** What does Leo do before checking the lamp with Maya?
**3A-4.** Why can't they complete the repair that day?
**3A-5.** Does the text say Maya has bought the replacement part?
**3A-6.** What suggests that Maya's attitude has changed?

## 4. Decide what to do with unknown words

Четыре решения: ignore — пропустить, guess — предположить, check later — проверить позже, look up now — проверить сейчас. Выбор зависит от задачи. Предположение можно уточнить после первого чтения.

The same word may require a different decision for a different task. For the main idea, you might understand `switch` simply as “a part of the lamp”. If you need to name the damaged part, you should check its exact meaning.

**4A-1.** You don't know “sewing”. You only need the main idea. Choose an action and explain why.
**4A-2.** You don't know “switch”. You need to name the part Maya must replace. Choose an action.
**4A-3.** Guess “loose” from the chair paragraph: securely attached, not securely attached, or very expensive?
**4A-4.** What might “that was not really the point” mean here? Explain the man's reason in your own words.

Проверь ответы к этому блоку после своей попытки. Значения sewing, switch и loose даны в ключе; заучивать их сейчас не нужно.

## 5. Use the story

**5A-1.** Write 3–4 English sentences: What did Maya gain, even though the lamp was still broken? Would you return? Explain. Use simple language if you need it.

Check: Did I explain the change? Did I give a reason? Is my meaning clear? You may look back at the story.

## 6. Lesson Check

Read this new paragraph without a dictionary:

> Tom borrowed a camera for a weekend trip. On Sunday evening, he noticed a tiny scratch on its case. The pictures were fine and every button worked, but he felt uncomfortable. Before returning it, he sent the owner a photo of the mark and asked how to put things right.

**6A-1.** Is the main problem a camera that cannot take pictures, or Tom's concern about damage to borrowed property?
**6A-2.** What evidence shows that the camera still works?
**6A-3.** If you don't know “case”, must you look it up to answer the first question? Explain.
**6A-4.** Summarise the situation in one sentence, in English or Russian.

Check your answers. The important questions are whether you understood the main problem, found evidence and made a sensible dictionary decision. This short check tests the reading strategy, not your overall English level.

## 7. Takeaway and next step

You can understand a story before you understand every word. A dictionary decision should serve your reading goal.

Если основная мысль потерялась, перечитай абзацы и выдели «кто → чего хотел → что произошло → что изменилось».

Before the next lesson, close the text and briefly recall what Maya wanted, what happened, and what changed.
