## Lesson

- **2A-1:** B.
- **2A-2:** No. They lack the right part; the lamp is “exactly as broken as before”.
- **3A-1:** Whether someone would repair it for her or she would do the work herself.
- **3A-2:** It belonged to his daughter and his grandson could use it. Emotional/family value is a reasonable inference; the text does not explicitly say he cannot afford a new chair.
- **3A-3:** He asks what happened and what she has already tried.
- **3A-4:** They do not have the right replacement switch.
- **3A-5:** No. Leo shows her how to find it; a purchase is not reported.
- **3A-6:** She passes the shop and wants to help repair her lamp. Other answers need evidence.
- **4A-1:** Suggested: ignore or check later; the exact activity is not needed for gist. “Sewing” means шитьё / шить. Looking it up later for interest is also reasonable.
- **4A-2:** Look up now if the task requires the exact part. A switch is выключатель. For gist alone, “a damaged part” could be enough.
- **4A-3:** Not securely attached — шатающийся / неплотно закреплённый.
- **4A-4:** Buying a replacement is possible, but keeping this particular family chair matters. This is an inference supported by his explanation.
- **5A-1:** Open. Check that the response identifies knowledge/participation, gives a personal decision and reason, and is understandable. Model: “Maya learned what was wrong with her lamp. She also learned that she could help repair it. I would return because I would like to finish the job.” A different personal decision is equally valid.
- **6A-1:** Tom's concern about damage to borrowed property.
- **6A-2:** “The pictures were fine and every button worked.”
- **6A-3:** Suggested: no; the camera works and there is a mark on it, enough for the main problem. “Case” here means the camera's outer body. Award no automatic penalty for a justified different dictionary choice.
- **6A-4:** Suggested: “Tom notices a small mark on a borrowed camera and contacts its owner to explain and resolve it.” Criteria: borrowed camera, damage/mark, contact with owner; no invented outcome.

## Additional practice

- **P1A-1:** b → d → a → c.
- **P1A-2:** They examine it together but cannot finish the repair that day.
- **P1A-3:** Interest in learning; she wants to help make it work. “Never buy anything new” is unsupported.
- **P1A-4:** She gained understanding and motivation despite no immediate physical repair.
- **P2A-1:** Choose some ground and start planting.
- **P2A-2:** The main tap was broken.
- **P2A-3:** Slightly wet; the contrast with dry helps.
- **P2A-4:** Suggested: both gain knowledge without the expected result; Maya investigates an object, Nina learns to observe plants. Accept supported alternatives.
- **P2A-5:** Open. “Tap” is useful if it prevents understanding why water comes from another building. No lookup is needed if that meaning is already understood. Evaluate reasoning, not a fixed word.
- **P3A-1:** Open. Criteria: a shared idea, evidence from both stories, coherent English, cautious scope. Model: “Both stories show people learning something different from what they expected. Maya wants a working lamp, but she learns how to investigate its problem. Nina wants vegetables, but she learns to check the soil. Neither person gets the original result immediately. However, both want to continue. In these situations, understanding the process seems to matter as well as getting a finished result.” Other interpretations with evidence are valid.
